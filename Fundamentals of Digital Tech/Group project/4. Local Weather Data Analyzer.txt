4. Local Weather Data Analyzer
Common Trunk Abstract
Analyze historical weather data to discover patterns, understand climate trends, and make
informed predictions.
Common Trunk (Group Work)
• Load weather dataset (temperature, rainfall, humidity from CSV)
• Clean data and handle missing values
• Calculate basic statistics (min, max, average temperature per month)
• Create simple line plots for temperature over time
Common Trunk Example
The group loads weather_data.csv with 5 years of daily data: date, temp_celsius,
rainfall_mm, humidity_percent. They build functions: load_data(),
clean_missing_values() (replace with monthly average), monthly_stats(), and
plot_temperature(). For January 2024: min -2°C, max 8°C, average 3°C.
Student A: Seasonal Pattern Detector with Visualizations
Individual Abstract: Identify seasonal patterns by grouping data into seasons (spring,
summer, autumn, winter) and comparing temperature, rainfall, and sunshine patterns.
Create visualizations showing which months are best for outdoor activities.
Individual Example: Student A groups 5 years of data by season: Spring (Mar-May): Avg
temp 12°C, rainfall 45mm/month, 15 rainy days/month (50% probability). Summer (Jun-
Aug): Avg temp 24°C, rainfall 25mm/month, 8 rainy days/month (26% probability).
Autumn (Sep-Nov): Avg temp 14°C, rainfall 60mm/month, 18 rainy days/month (60%
probability). Winter (Dec-Feb): Avg temp 4°C, rainfall 55mm/month, 16 rainy
days/month (53% probability). Box plots show: summer has least variability (std dev 2°C)
while spring is most unpredictable (std dev 6°C). Heatmap shows: hottest month is July
(avg 26°C), coldest is January (avg 2°C). Rainfall pattern: driest in July (18mm), wettest in
October (75mm). Recommendation for tourism board: “Best months for outdoor events:
June, July, August (74% chance of dry weather, average 24°C). Avoid October (very rainy).”
The visualizer creates a 12-month summary chart showing temperature (line) and rainfall
(bars) overlaid, making patterns obvious at a glance.
Student B: Rain Prediction System
Individual Abstract: Build a probabilistic model to predict likelihood of rain based on
current weather conditions (humidity, pressure, temperature). Use historical correlations
to make predictions.
Individual Example: Student B analyzes 1,825 days of data. They find correlations: High
humidity (>80%) + Low pressure (<1010 hPa): Rain occurred 85% of the time (156 out
of 183 days). High humidity + Normal pressure (1010-1020): Rain occurred 45% of the
time. Low humidity (<60%): Rain occurred only 8% of the time. Today’s conditions:
Humidity 82%, Pressure 1008 hPa, Temperature 18°C. The prediction model calculates:
Conditions match “high humidity + low pressure” pattern → 85% probability of rain.
Recommendation: “Bring an umbrella! Strong chance of rain today.” The model also
considers: Temperature < 0°C and humidity high → snow instead of rain (occurred 90% of
the time in historical data). Another example: Humidity 55%, Pressure 1018 hPa → Pattern
matches “low humidity + normal pressure” → 12% probability of rain. Recommendation:
“Clear skies expected!” Student B creates a confusion matrix to validate: Model predicted
rain correctly 80% of the time, predicted no-rain correctly 75% of the time (good
accuracy!).
Student C: Temperature Trend Analyzer (Climate Change Detection)
Individual Abstract: Use linear regression to analyze temperature trends over multiple
years. Determine if temperatures are increasing, decreasing, or stable. Quantify climate
change in your local area.
Individual Example: Student C analyzes summer temperatures (Jun-Aug) over 20 years:
2005: 22.1°C avg, 2010: 22.8°C, 2015: 23.5°C, 2020: 24.2°C, 2025: 24.9°C. Linear
regression: y = 0.14x + 22.0 (where x = years since 2005). The slope (+0.14°C per year)
means temperatures are rising by 1.4°C per decade! Visualization: scatter plot with trend
line clearly shows upward slope. Statistical test: correlation coefficient r = 0.91 (strong
positive correlation). P-value = 0.003 (statistically significant, not random variation).
Prediction: By 2030, summer average will be 25.9°C (2.4°C warmer than 2005). Student C
also analyzes winter temperatures: 2005: 3.8°C, 2025: 5.1°C. Trend: +0.065°C/year (slower
warming but still increasing). Rainfall trend: -2mm/year (getting drier). Conclusion: “Our
region is experiencing measurable climate change: summers 1.4°C warmer per decade,
winters slightly warmer, and decreasing rainfall. This is consistent with global warming
patterns.” Comparison with global data (if available) shows local warming rate is faster
than global average (+0.14°C vs +0.08°C per year).
Student D: Weather Anomaly Detector
Individual Abstract: Identify unusual weather events (heat waves, cold snaps, storms)
using statistical methods. Calculate standard deviations and flag days that are significantly
different from normal patterns.
Individual Example: Student D calculates monthly normals: July average = 25°C, std dev =
2°C. Definition of anomaly: any day more than 2 standard deviations from mean. July 15,
2024: Temperature = 35°C. Z-score = (35 - 25) ÷ 2 = +5.0 (extreme heat wave! 5 standard
deviations above normal). January 20, 2024: Temperature = -12°C. January normal = 3°C,
std dev = 3°C. Z-score = (-12 - 3) ÷ 3 = -5.0 (extreme cold snap!). Over 5 years, the detector
finds: 12 heat waves (z-score > +2), 8 cold snaps (z-score < -2), 25 heavy rainfall days
(>50mm in 24 hours, z-score > +3), 3 record-breaking days (hottest/coldest ever).
Visualization: Calendar heatmap showing anomalies in red (hot) and blue (cold). Timeline
shows anomalies are becoming more frequent: 2020 had 2 heat waves, 2024 had 5 heat
waves (climate change impact?). The system also detects: “Unusual pattern: 3 consecutive
days above 30°C in April (historically rare, only happened twice in 20 years).” Real-time
alerting: “Today’s forecast shows 36°C—this would be a record high for June. Stay
hydrated!”
